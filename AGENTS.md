# tiny-fps

Tiny multiplayer first-person game for the browser: a rooftop capture-the-flag with parkour movement, in progress (see [docs/PLAN.md](docs/PLAN.md)). A personal project on `korial29`: never move or mirror it to an organisation. Tooling, CI and the transport layer come from `korial29/air-hockey` (`~/dev/air-hockey`): when in doubt, do what air-hockey does.

Read this file before any change. Change a gameplay rule only when the user asks.

## Commands

Node 24 (`.nvmrc`).

```bash
npm start           # demo with hot reload (Vite)
npm run dev:server  # WebSocket server on :8787
npm test            # Vitest
npm run lint        # Prettier + ESLint + tsc --noEmit
npm run build       # dist/: client, local, server, types
```

## Size comes first

- Budgets, gated by `npm run size`: `dist/index.js` ≤ 164 KB gzip, `dist/local.js` ≤ 40 KB (see `scripts/size.mjs`) (raised by the user: from 12 and 6 for parity with the js13k game, from 30 and 12 for level design, then the client from 40 for the gun, from 48 for the katana and from 110 for the space trooper's armour). Raise one only when the user agrees.
- No game or 3D library, no runtime dependency, no images, fonts or assets: geometry and textures are generated in code.
- Raw WebGL2: one scene program (depth passes included) and one small post-processing program. No three.js, no WASM.
- Do not copy code from `carlini/js13k2019-yet-another-doom-clone`: it is GPL-3.0, this project is Apache-2.0. Its ideas (procedural textures, maps as compact data, shadow maps, jsfxr-style sound) are fair game; reimplement them.

## Architecture

- **The host is authoritative**: the Node server or the local host tab.
- **Inputs, not positions**: the client sends one `Input` per step (12 bytes, 60 Hz): held keys (10 bits: forward, back, left, right, jump, sprint, dash, crouch, fire, blade), yaw, pitch, a u16 `seq`, and the `view` tick it draws the others at. The host applies each input on arrival with `move()` and acks the last seq per player in the snapshot. A bank of `INPUT_BURST` inputs per player stops speed hacks.
- **The client predicts** its own player: it applies its inputs at once, and on each snapshot resets to the host state and replays the unacked inputs. Other players are drawn `INTERP_DELAY` (100 ms) in the past, interpolated between snapshots.
- **Snapshots** at 30 Hz (`SNAP_EVERY` 2 of `TICK_HZ` 60): a 4-byte head (type, tick, player count), 47 bytes per player (position, velocity, the grabbed ledge, look angles, the last acked seq, the movement mode, the time in it, the wall side, the jumps and dash left and their timers, the stumble timer, and the gun and the katana: hp, cool, ammo, down, blade, wield, zap, the swing and its cut, the shot and hit counters: all a `step()` needs to continue from; the spare bits of the dash and stun bytes carry the id of the player who tackled, `SnapPlayer.by`, which is no part of the movement; the last byte is the limb a finishing cut took off, `lost`, for the drawing), then the match: 8 bytes (captures of red and blue, the clock in whole seconds with an over bit, the packs there as a u32 bitmask: the health packs first, then the ammo packs) and one state byte per flag, with the carrier's id when carried and the resting place and the seconds left when dropped. Both flags home is 10 bytes over the players. Then the plasma balls, only when any flies: a count byte and 10 bytes a ball (id, shooter and burst bit, place in 1/32 m, yaw and pitch in a byte each); a burst ball is sent once more, where it burst. The end of a match is a text message (`end`: the score and each player's name, team, captures and returns).
- **Maps and the host**: the room plays `MAPS[rules.map]` (`maps.ts`: `highline`, `tranquility`), or the `WorldDef` given to its constructor. The host is the player in the room the longest (`Player.since`); only his `{ t: 'map', map }` counts, kept as `next` and loaded (`Room.load()`: new world, `Ctf`, packs, history) at the next match start or when the room empties, never mid-match. The roster carries `map`, `host` and `next`. The client's `tinyFps()` owns the socket; `play()` is the scene of one map behind a socket of its own, and a roster with another map destroys it and mounts the new map on the same connection (no second join). The host's buttons are the HUD's `menu`, in the title screen's Game panel. There is no level progression. Health, the gun, the racks and the packs are the weapon (docs/WEAPON.md, below).
- **Highline** (`map.ts`, step 9): 94 by 240 m, a 26 m tower at each end (flag, eight spawns, cover), three routes between them at three heights and speeds, the plaza in the middle (see docs/PLAN.md, the step 9 rules, for the layout). To change it: edit red's `half` or the `centre` list, nothing else; the blue side is the flip. Keep a crack between two brushes either closed or at least a body wide, keep every ledge to climb 2.2 m or less above the roof it is caught from, and keep a metre of air between a ledge and the roof next to it so the hanging body has room. The tests fly every route with the real physics (`test/routes.ts`: legs of `go`, `leap` and `slide`, flown by `test/pilot.ts`), so a move of a brush shows at once as a leg that no longer lands; change a leg's numbers only after looking at where the player really lands.
- **Characters** (`BODY` in `types.ts`, `BUILD` in `rig.ts`): the man (0) or the woman (1), picked on the join screen (`TinyFpsOptions.body`, the join frame's `body`, `PlayerInfo.body` in the roster, absent for the man so an old roster is unchanged). The look only: the simulation, the hit cylinder and the eye height do not know it. Her skeleton is `RESTS[1]` (a `Pose` carries its `rest`; copies must keep it): the man's legs under a trunk 3 cm shorter, narrower shoulders, shorter arms. Her skin is the man's vertex for vertex (`retarget()` in `skin.ts`: volumes shaped on his rest mesh, then each joint's `SCALE` about its bind), kept as `Skin.u` so `armor.ts` cuts the same plates on her. Her face is carved by the same functions with `fem` on; her hair is `hair.ts` (five styles by id, on the head joint, nothing under a helmet). `Renderer.wear()`, `Body.wear()` and `Hud.wear()` take the roster's choice; a function that takes a build as a second optional argument must not be passed to `map` (the index would be the build). On the moon she wears her own helmet (`helm.ts`: a smooth dome, a dark T visor framed in the team's colour, grey cheek plates, a crest in the team's colour, ear caps and a stalk on the right one; no hose), and her chest plate has a cup each side (`bust()` and `CUP` in `armor.ts`, over `BUST` in `skin.ts`): the user asked for both, to tell her from him at a glance. Her shoulders have no pad standing over them: a cap cut out of the skin of each deltoid (the `fem` regions of `armor.ts`), which follows the shoulder.
- **Title screen** (`hud.ts`, the `<aside>` shown without the pointer lock): a panel over the dimmed view, with a menu on the left (`TABS`: Game, Controls), the Game panel (the character cards `CAST`, the mode in `GOAL`'s lines, the map's `name`, `tag` and `lore` from its `WorldDef`, the host's `menu`) and the Controls panel built from `CONTROLS`, whose codes must cover `CODES` of `input.ts` (a test says so: a new key goes in both). The panel takes its own clicks; its orange button (`createHud`'s `play`) and the view round it start the game. A card's picture is `Renderer.portrait()`: a frame of the own player's runner alone in the map's sky, copied into the card's 2D canvas before the real frame is drawn, no image file. A card chosen sends `{ t: 'body', body }`: the room changes `PlayerInfo.body` at once and sends the roster (an older host ignores the frame, and its next roster puts the card back); `local/` keeps it in the join it replays to a new host.
- **Teams**: red (0) and blue (1) from `TEAM`. A joiner goes to the smaller team, red on a tie; nobody is moved when someone leaves, the next joiner fills the smaller side. `PlayerInfo.team` and `color` (the team's colour, so the runner code needs nothing) are in the roster. A team spawns at its own `WorldDef.teams[team].spawns`, and so does a fall: `move()` takes the team as its last argument and the client's predictor gets it from `you(id, team)`. A fall puts `EV.RESPAWN` in `ev`, which the host reads.
- **Capture the flag** (`ctf.ts`, ticked by the room at `TICK_HZ`): a flag is home (on its team's base), carried (by a player id) or dropped (a point and a timer). A player touches a flag within `FLAG_REACH` (1.5 m) sideways, with his body overlapping its `FLAG_H` height. An enemy takes a flag at home or lying; a teammate sends a lying one home at once (a returner is counted), before an enemy can take it; a lying flag goes home after `FLAG_RETURN` (10 s). A carrier at his own base while his own flag is home scores one and the carried flag goes home. A carrier who falls under the kill floor drops it where he last stood on a floor, and so does one who leaves. `Room.drop(id)` is the hook of the tackle (step 7): the flag falls under the carrier (on the floor below him in the air, at his last ground over the void), and he cannot take it back for `FLAG_LOCK` (2 s), nor can anyone send it home: it lies there, and the carrier's teammates may pick it up (the user's choice). The flags never change a `PlayerState`, so prediction is the same with or without them. A tackler who stands by the flag he made drop sends it home at once, as any teammate touching a lying flag does.
- **A match** runs to `rules.scoreLimit` (3) captures or `rules.timeLimit` (480 s), the most captures winning and a level score a draw. Then the end screen runs `END_SCREEN` (10 s), the scores and flags reset and everyone is put back at a spawn of his team. An empty room starts from scratch. The client reads takes, drops, returns, captures and new matches off two snapshots in a row (`beats.ts`) for the banners and the sounds.
- **Movement** (`physics.ts`, constants in `constants.ts`): `move()` is one step of a state machine over `MODE` (ground, air, wall-run, hang, climb, slide, crouch). Ground control accelerates toward the wished direction, kills the speed across it (`GRIP`) and has friction; the sprint is slow acceleration past `RUN_SPEED`. A dash is a burst (`DASH_TICKS`, no gravity), once on the ground or once in the air per landing, with a cooldown. Jump, dash and the rest are presses (edges on `held`, not holds); a press is kept `JUMP_BUFFER_TICKS` and a jump works `COYOTE_TICKS` after an edge. A ledge is caught in the air on a side the player faces, a top `GRAB_MIN` to `GRAB_MAX` above the feet with room above it; a wall-run starts in the air with forward held, from `WALLRUN_MIN_SPEED` (a normal run is enough) toward or along a wall within `WALL_REACH`, at most `WALL_PARALLEL` of the speed into it: the velocity is turned along the wall (`WALL_TURN_KEEP` of the speed), lifted, and the body is drawn in to the wall (`WALL_PULL`). The state is float32 and `Math.hypot` is not used, so host and client agree to the bit. The animation reads `PlayerState.ev` (`EV` bits of the last step) and `fall`, not sent; the predictor's `events()` hands the live ones out once. Keys: WASD, Shift, Space, C; a dash is a move key pressed twice within `DOUBLE_TAP_MS` (250 ms, `input.ts`): the step after the second press carries the DASH bit and only the tapped direction (`Controls.take()`), so the burst goes that way whatever else is held, with no wire change.
- **Brush world** (`world.ts`, `physics.ts`): solid axis-aligned boxes, floors are tops, walls are sides, boxes may float. `build()` lists each brush in the 4 m grid cells its footprint covers; physics asks `near()` for the few candidates, `ray()` walks the cells the ray crosses with an exact slab test. The player is a cylinder: it slides along box sides, steps onto a top at most `STEP_H` above the feet, stands on the highest top under its circle, bumps its head on bottoms, and goes back to the spawn of its id in its team under `def.floor`. Sides are judged from the feet at the start of the step. A spawn is `[x, y, z, yaw]`.
- **Lag compensation history** (`room.ts`): every snapshot tick the room keeps the players' states for `MAX_REWIND` ticks. `rewind(id, view)` answers the other players as player `id` drew them at the `view` tick of one of its inputs, interpolated the way the client does. The tackle calls it (see Tackle below). `ray()` and `hidden()` (`world.ts`) are the generic ray and line of sight through the brushes, for the same use.
- **Tackle** (`tackle.ts`, `Room.tackle()`, `stumble()` in `physics.ts`): after each input it applies, the room asks whether that player is tackling (in a slide, or in the burst of a dash) and overlaps an enemy carrier (`reaches()`: cylinders, `TACKLE_REACH` 0.3 m of reach), the carrier taken from `rewind(id, input.view)`, so a tackle that hit on the tackler's screen counts at the host. The first carrier hit drops the flag (`drop()`) and `stumble()`s: `PlayerState.stun` = `STUMBLE_TICKS` (48, 0.8 s) during which `move()` drops the jump and dash presses and holds the speed to `STUMBLE_SPEED`, after a throw of `STUMBLE_KNOCK` along the tackler's way that slides out at `STUMBLE_DRAG`; a dash burst, a hang, a climb and a wall-run end. Only enemy carriers, and the tackler waits `TACKLE_COOLDOWN` (30 ticks). The stumble is in the snapshot, so the tackled client predicts it exactly once the snapshot with it arrives (a tackle cannot be predicted before). Only the host calls `stumble()`. The client reads a tackle as a stumble timer that went up between two snapshots (`beats.ts`, `tackle` beat): the tackler gets a banner, a punch (`effects.punch()`) and an impact; the one tackled a jolt (`effects.jolt()`), the red vignette (`hud.hurt()`), an impact and a grunt; everyone else an impact at the spot; the runner staggers (`soldier.ts`) and the own arms flail (`body.ts`) while `stun` runs.
- **The gun** (`gun.ts`, `plasma.ts`, `Room.shoot()`, `Room.heal()`, docs/WEAPON.md): `step()` runs with every input on the host and in the predictor, so the own `ammo`, `cool` and `shots` are predicted exactly; the team's rack (`TeamDef.rack`, within `RACK_R`) is predicted too: it fills the gun on arrival only, and `PlayerState.rack` (in the snapshot) tells the step whether the player was already inside. A respawn clears it. A shot (`FIRE` held, `cool` 0, `ammo` > 0, not hanging or climbing, match not over) is a plasma ball (`plasma.ts`) from the eye of the mode along the input's yaw and pitch, no spread, at `PLASMA_SPEED`: the room first flies it by the shooter's lag (the input's view tick to now, at most `PLASMA_LAG` ticks), then a tick at a time, bursting on the first brush or enemy who is up; the shooter's client flies its own from the predicted step and takes the host's copy only for its burst (`balls.ts`). A hit takes 1 hp and counts in the shooter's `hits`. At 0 hp `down` = `DOWN_TICKS`, the gun goes and a carried flag drops with the tackle's lock; `step()` moves a player who is down with no keys and counts `down` per input, so the predictor replays it exactly from the snapshot, and at 0 puts him back at a spawn of his team (`respawn()`, which keeps `shots` and `hits`: a reset would read as a shot). Health and packs are the host's alone: `hp` never feeds back into movement, so the predictor leaves it to the snapshot. A health pack (`WorldDef.packs`) heals `PACK_HP` a player below `HP_MAX` within `PACK_R`; an ammo pack (`WorldDef.ammo`) gives `AMMO_PACK` shots, up to `AMMO_MAX`, to a player who has the gun and is not full. Each is gone `PACK_BACK` s, and `Snapshot.packs` holds the bits of the ones there (health packs, then ammo packs, 32 at most). An ammo pack changes `ammo` between two inputs like a hit changes `hp`: it is the host's alone, and the predictor replays its unacked inputs on the snapshot's ammo. A lying player touches no flag.
- **Grenade launcher** (`plasma.ts`, `gun.ts` `step()`, `Room.fly()`, docs/WEAPON.md): the laser rifle's under-barrel launcher. G (`KEY.SPECIAL`, FIRE not needed) with the rifle in hand spends one of `NADE_MAX` grenades (the rifle's rack loads them), `NADE_COOL` ticks apart: a `Ball` of power `NADE` with a speed (`Ball.v`), flown a tick at a time by `toss()` under the map's gravity, bursting on the first brush or enemy or at the end of `NADE_RANGE`. Its burst takes `blast()` from every enemy who is up and from its owner, never from a teammate, never through a brush. `nades` and `lob` are predicted by `step()`; the shooter's client flies its own grenade with `toss()`. One byte more a player on the wire (62), and the ball's power byte carries the speed.
- **Katana** (`blade.ts`, `gun.ts` `step()`, `Room.cut()`, docs/WEAPON.md): the team's `TeamDef.blade` rack (within `RACK_R`, no ammo) gives `blade`, kept until he goes down; `wield` (in hand) is `blade` and (`KEY.BLADE` held, or no gun, or a swing running), computed by `step()` and predicted exactly. `FIRE` with the katana in hand and `swing` 0, not hanging or climbing, starts a swing of `CUTS[cut].ticks` (`cutOf(keys)`: LEFT or RIGHT alone across, else FORWARD thrust, else slash) and the gun does not fire. `step()` returns `STRIKE.CUT` on the step where `ticks - swing === strike`, and a caller must never take it for a shot (`STRIKE.SHOT`). The room resolves it against the enemies as the attacker drew them (`rewind()`, not down now or then): every one in `strike()` loses `CUTS[cut].hp` (2, 2, 2, 3); at 0 he goes down like a laser kill, else he is shocked, `stumble(SHOCK_TICKS, SHOCK_KNOCK)` along `knock()` with `zap` true and `by` the attacker, and a carried flag stays. A tackle's stumble sets `zap` false, `step()` clears it with `stun`. `hits` counts once per cut that hits anyone. Going down or respawning clears `blade`, `wield` and `swing`. The wire: `blade`, `wield`, `zap` are bits 21 to 23 of the gun field, `swing` (6 bits) and `cut` (2) one byte more per player. A cut that puts him down sets `PlayerState.lost` to `limbOf()` of the foe as drawn (docs/WEAPON.md, limb cuts); going down to a shot sets it NONE and so does a respawn (`STILL`); it is the last byte of the player record, bits 0-2.
- **Look** (step 8, Mirror's Edge): white precast `CONCRETE` roofs and walls, dark blue `GLASS` whose reflection of the sky is added in the shader (`K0`, the sky function, along the reflected view with a Fresnel term, over the strongest specular row), brushed `STEEL`, near white `PAINT` that takes the brush tint as its colour; brick and wood stay for variety and ROOFTOPS uses one brick facade. **Runner vision** (`vision.ts`, pure, from the brushes alone): red geometry 1 cm proud of the surface, appended to the map's vertices: a band along the top edges of boxes a runner can grab or climb (ground beside the edge more than `STEP_H` and at most 2.4 m below, edge at least 1.5 m), pipes and beams (the two smaller sides at most 0.6 m) red all over and lit, a stripe at hand height on the long faces of a free-standing wall (at least 8 m long and 2.5 m high, at most 3 m thick); bands and stripes are layer -2 (unlit, they bloom). `zones()` paints a square in the team colour under each base. **Street** (`street.ts`): the ground of the city is `STREET` = -80, about 100 m under the roofs. A grid of 16 m roads comes from the play area's bounds: two hug it at 10 m, the others follow every 72 m out to 150 m past the circle round it. The play area is one block: asphalt in its gaps, paving 3 m round each building. `city()` in `render.ts` takes every brush that goes down to the floor on down to the street (`deepen()`) and adds the skyline, `scenery()` adds `street()`: flat lit quads for the paving, the asphalt, a dashed yellow centre line, white edge lines and crosswalks. **Skyline** (`skyline.ts`): seeded towers in the other blocks, one to a lot of about 40 m, glass, steel or concrete, some with a coloured cap; near the play area they stay under its lowest roof. `build()` and the physics never see any of it (tests pin it). **Traffic** (`traffic.ts`): 64 cars on the two roads each side of the play area, on both axes, a pure function of the wall clock (`Date.now()`, so every client sees the same ones, with no network). Each axis of roads has its own green: a car hops from one stop line to the next on it with an ease and waits there during the other axis' green, so no two cars meet at a crossing and a queue keeps its order; at the end of its road it shrinks away and comes back at the other end. Two white templates (body and cabin, tinted by `place()`) and one of unlit glowing lamps, drawn each frame for the cars in front of the camera within 260 m, with the cabin within 90 m. The sky, sun, haze, a cool grade and the bloom are in the world's `sun`, `sky`, `fog` and the post program; the shade is blue-grey (`daylight()`). The fog is a height fog: the world's density plus a layer of `FOG_LAYER` (2 %) of it at the lowest roof that grows by e every 20 m down (three times the haze on the street), integrated along the view ray in closed form in the scene shader (`fogDepth()` is its twin for the tests).
- **Faces** (`features.ts`, `expression.ts`, the head in `skin.ts`): the head is a carved surface with soft colour edges (each corner blends two palette colours: hairline, brows, beard, lips); the eyes are a ball, an iris that slides on it and two lids, sheets of their own. A face is 27 weights (`CH`): 15 of expression, 12 traits of the player (`lookOf(id)`, with `toneOf`, `hairOf`, `eyesOf`, `beardOf`: the same on every screen). `createFace(id).update(dt, mood)` blends the expressions in the order of `LAYERS` (effort, focus, grin, shout, wince, down: the last wins), blinks every 2 to 6 s and keeps the irises on the aim from where the head points (`soldier()` gives it the head's solved turn). To add an expression: a `POSE` and a `LAYERS` row, and its frame in the HUD's `face.ts` (`frame()`, `mood()`). To add a channel: a `CH` id and its field in `shift()`; `skin.ts` bakes it. Levels: close and near carry the channels (5768 and 1826 vertices skinned), far has none (327: a mark for each eye, brow and the mouth, and a nose). Under the moon helmet's dark visor no face shows and `soldier()` skips the channels; a head a katana took off shows its own. Nothing of it is in `src/shared`: no hit box, no snapshot byte.
- **Fists** (`PUNCH` in `constants.ts`, `step()` in `gun.ts`, `Room.punch()`): with no weapon at all (no gun, katana, rail gun or rifle) FIRE throws a punch, and the next when it is over while held: `swing` runs `PUNCH.ticks` (20) with no `blade`, which is how a client tells a punch from a cut; no byte more on the wire. It strikes at tick 6 through the katana's strike test with its own reach (1.4 m) and arc. A punch is half a point: the host counts them per victim (`bruise`) and every second one takes 1 hp. The third a player takes with no more than `PUNCH_MEMORY` (3 s) between two stuns him as a tackle does (stumble, thrown the way the puncher faces, the flag dropped, `by` the puncher): the clients read it as a tackle beat. A weapon coming to hand ends a punch. Drawn: bare-handed runners stand in a karate guard and breathe (`FISTS`, `LOW_GUARD`, `BREATH` in `gait.ts`); the punch is the rear fist (`JAB`, `thrown()`), in the own view too (`body.ts`).
- **Lighting** (`render.ts`): the sector light is a dim ambient; up to `MAX_LIGHTS` spots and points from `gather()` (`lights.ts`: the flashlights, the lava) add up in the one shader program, and the first `MAX_SHADOWS` shadowed spots get a depth pass into a shadow-map array (the same program, colour writes off). Lava and the unlit layer -2 light themselves. The lit scene goes into a 4x multisampled offscreen target (RGBA16F with `EXT_color_buffer_float`, RGBA8 otherwise), resolved to a texture; the post program adds a three-level bloom, a tone shoulder, a vignette, grain, a cool grade and gamma.
- **Vehicle damage** (`VEHICLE` in `constants.ts`, `Room.hurt()`, `wreck()`, `boom()`, `src/client/wreck.ts`, docs/MOON.md): a rover and a ship have 10 HP, the host's alone like a player's `hp` (no step reads it, the snapshot carries it in the vehicle's kind byte, the wreck's time in the next). Every weapon but the fists takes some from the hull of a vehicle that is not of the shooter's team (its driver's, else the base it is parked at); the hull stops the shot before the crew. At 0 it blows up: the crew goes down, a grenade's blast goes off, the wreck lies 5 s with no seat, then the vehicle is parked afresh. A player who is down does not collide with a wreck (`solid()`), on the host and in the predictor alike. The client reads everything off `hp` and `wreck` in two frames in a row: arcs by health, the explosion, the wreck, its crumbling, the HUD's `tt` bars.
- **`src/shared` never touches the DOM or Node APIs.**
- Coordinates: metres, y up, yaw 0 looks toward -z, `forward(yaw) = (-sin yaw, -cos yaw)` on x, z.
- Keys are read by `KeyboardEvent.code`: WASD on QWERTY is ZQSD on AZERTY with no extra code.
- `erasableSyntaxOnly` is on: no enums, no parameter properties, no namespaces.

## Tests

- Vitest. A fix comes with a test that fails without it.
- Prediction must be checked against the room: the same inputs through the room and through the predictor end at exactly the same place (state is float32 on both sides). Those tests build their world from `ROOFTOPS` and pass it to the `Room`, whose default map is `HIGHLINE`.
- The map is checked by `src/shared/highline.test.ts`: symmetry (every brush has a twin, bases and spawns mirror), validity (no overlap, no crack, spawns and flags on tops with room), the three routes from each base to the other flag and back, the fast route's need for a dash, a double jump and a wall-run and the low route's need for none, the ways back up after a miss, and a capture flown through a room.
- Visual work: render the built client with a fake socket in headless Chromium, look at the screenshot, then report.

## Git, PRs and releases

- Same flow as air-hockey: branch from `master` (`feature/…`, `fix/…`), one release label per PR, rebase on `origin/master` with `--force-with-lease`, conventional commit subjects in English.
- The PR's label picks the bump (`release:major`, `release:minor`, `release:patch`); `release:skip` publishes nothing. A merge releases at once, with no approval (single maintainer): npm publish, `vX.Y.Z` tag, GitHub Release, built from the PR's merge commit, one release at a time.

## Working with several agents

- The shared contract (types, constants, signatures) is committed first.
- Each agent gets its own worktree from that commit and owns its files; merge by hand, run every check, then look at a screenshot.

## Writing

- English for code, comments, docs, commits and PRs.
- Comments state the non-obvious fact and stop.
