# tiny-fps

Tiny multiplayer first-person game for the browser: a rooftop capture-the-flag with parkour movement, in progress (see [docs/PLAN.md](docs/PLAN.md)). A personal project on `korial29`: never move or mirror it to an organisation. Tooling, CI and the transport layer come from `korial29/air-hockey` (`~/dev/air-hockey`): when in doubt, do what air-hockey does.

Read this file before any change. Change a gameplay rule only when the user asks.

## Frame time comes next

Read [docs/PERFORMANCE.md](docs/PERFORMANCE.md) before adding anything drawn every frame. Every runner, the own body and the vehicles are rebuilt on the CPU each frame, so the cost is per vertex; the JavaScript of a match frame stays under 8 ms. In short:

- Count the vertices of what you add at every level (`CLOSE` 4 m, `NEAR` 10 m, far beyond, most runners far); a piece on a runner takes the level, its far version a quarter of the vertices or fewer. Say the counts in the PR.
- Build once at rest and only carry it per frame; cache what depends on a few numbers (`hand()`); work per skin vertex, not per triangle corner (`dress()`).
- No allocation in a loop over vertices: no array literals, destructuring, `map`, spread there; plain `const` numbers, writes by index.
- Write into the frame's `Verts` (`verts.ts`): `out: Out`, `room(out, n)` then writes by index and `out.length = w`; never a new array per object. In the game a per-frame model gets only the `Verts`, never a plain array (that turned a 9 ms frame into 65 ms in Chrome); tests may pass `[]`.
- No first-use cost in a frame of a match: prepare it when the map loads (`createRenderer()`, `Renderer.wear()`).
- A pure speed-up changes no float: compare every vertex before and after. A level of detail is shown from its distance, before and after.

## Architecture

- **The host is authoritative**: the Node server or the local host tab.
- **Inputs, not positions**: the client sends one `Input` per step (12 bytes, 60 Hz): held keys (10 bits: forward, back, left, right, jump, sprint, dash, crouch, fire, blade), yaw, pitch, a u16 `seq`, and the `view` tick it draws the others at. The host applies each input on arrival with `move()` and acks the last seq per player in the snapshot. A bank of `INPUT_BURST` inputs per player stops speed hacks.
- **The client predicts** its own player: it applies its inputs at once, and on each snapshot resets to the host state and replays the unacked inputs. Other players are drawn `INTERP_DELAY` (100 ms) in the past, interpolated between snapshots.
- **Snapshots** at 30 Hz (`SNAP_EVERY` 2 of `TICK_HZ` 60): a 5-byte head (type, tick, player count, vehicle count: a byte each since Tranquility's eight vehicles, `MAX_ROVERS` 15), 47 bytes per player (position, velocity, the grabbed ledge, look angles, the last acked seq, the movement mode, the time in it, the wall side, the jumps and dash left and their timers, the stumble timer, and the gun and the katana: hp, cool, ammo, down, blade, wield, zap, the swing and its cut, the shot and hit counters: all a `step()` needs to continue from; the spare bits of the dash and stun bytes carry the id of the player who tackled, `SnapPlayer.by`, which is no part of the movement; the last byte is the limb a finishing cut took off, `lost`, for the drawing; while he is down the gun's `charge` byte, 0 then, carries how he fell instead, `fell`: the `FALL` in bits 5-6, the hit's way in 32nds of a turn in bits 0-4, `felled()` in `gun.ts`, decoded by `down`, so no byte more), then the match: 8 bytes (captures of red and blue, the clock in whole seconds with an over bit, the packs there as a u32 bitmask: the health packs first, then the ammo packs) and one state byte per flag, with the carrier's id when carried and the resting place and the seconds left when dropped. Both flags home is 10 bytes over the players. Then the plasma balls, only when any flies: a count byte and 10 bytes a ball (id, shooter and burst bit, place in 1/32 m, yaw and pitch in a byte each); a burst ball is sent once more, where it burst. The end of a match is a text message (`end`: the score and each player's name, team, captures, frags and returns).
- **Maps and the host**: the room plays `MAPS[rules.map]` (`maps.ts`: `highline`, `tranquility`), or the `WorldDef` given to its constructor. The host is the player in the room the longest (`Player.since`); only his `{ t: 'map', map }` counts, kept as `next` and loaded (`Room.load()`: new world, `Ctf`, packs, history) at the next match start or when the room empties, never mid-match. The roster carries `map`, `host` and `next`. The client's `tinyFps()` owns the socket; `play()` is the scene of one map behind a socket of its own, and a roster with another map destroys it and mounts the new map on the same connection (no second join). The game's menu offers no map (the user, 2026-11-13): the HUD has no map picker, the map is the join page's choice (the room still takes a host's `{ t: 'map' }`). There is no level progression. Health, the gun, the racks and the packs are the weapon (docs/WEAPON.md, below).
- **Bots** (`bot.ts`, `nav.ts`, `Room.fill()`): `rules.bots` (`BOTS` on the server, the demo's Bots choice) fills the room up to that many players while a person is in it, the teams even: a bot comes on the smaller team and leaves the larger one, gives its place to a joiner and comes back when he leaves, and goes with the last person. A bot is a `Player` with a `bot` and a connection that drops what it is sent; the room calls `think()` for each in `tick()` and applies its input like a client's on arrival (`apply()`), so nothing on the wire changes. `PlayerInfo.bot` marks it in the roster, its name ends in "(bot)", it never hosts and `size` leaves it out. The bots take the marine, the ninja and the xenomorph in turn by name (`BOT_ROLES`; the moon's ninja is an astronaut, `roleOf()`); a bot is handed its `Kit` (`Sight.kit`): one whose class takes no gun (the xenomorph) skips the rack and runs at an enemy in sight within `HUNT`, and one that jumps unlike the marine steers every leap in the air to come down on its point, as the moon's `land` legs. An id a bot lets go of is not given again in the same tick. A bot plans its way only on a floor, keeps a leg's keys until it lands, and shoots only off a leg (a jump's run-up keeps its sprint); it steps aside while it shoots, never toward a drop, a carrier runs rather than fights, and a straight walk stops at the void or a drop that hurts (`FALL_SAFE`). The moon's three routes are the bridge, the west long jump and the east stones (`CROSSINGS` in `nav.ts`, a `leap` with `land` comes down on its point), so bots spread over the rille. They fight after UT2004's bots (docs/BOTS.md): a character per bot (`personaOf()`: aim, reaction, Jumpiness, strafing, ducking) scaled by `Rules.botSkill` (0 to 7, `BOT_SKILL` on the server, the demo's Level slider); `dodge()` strafes, jumps and dashes aside in a fight, `evade()` dashes across the way of an enemy ball that would pass within 1.3 m; `walk()` goes round walls and drops by the nearest open turn, jumps onto low boxes, dashes on long wide straights, turns its look a little each tick (a leg of a route and the carrier turn at once), and stands rather than step into the void. A change to them is judged on 24 bot-only matches or more against master: one match is noise. ## Tests

- Vitest. A fix comes with a test that fails without it.
- CI runs the tests on four runners (`ci.yml`), the files split by their seconds in `test/durations.json` (`test/sequencer.ts`), four workers each, no coverage. Keep a file under about 15 s locally: Vitest runs a file's tests one after another, so a long file is the floor of its shard; split a heavy one by its `describe` blocks into `name.topic.test.ts`. Run `npm run test:durations` after adding, splitting or growing a test file.
- Prediction must be checked against the room: the same inputs through the room and through the predictor end at exactly the same place (state is float32 on both sides). Those tests build their world from `ROOFTOPS` and pass it to the `Room`, whose default map is `HIGHLINE`.
- The map is checked by `src/shared/highline.test.ts`: symmetry (every brush has a twin, bases and spawns mirror), validity (no overlap, no crack, spawns and flags on tops with room), the three routes from each base to the other flag and back, the fast route's need for a dash, a double jump and a wall-run and the low route's need for none, the ways back up after a miss, and a capture flown through a room.
- Visual work: render the built client with a fake socket in headless Chromium, look at the screenshot, then report.
- 3D rendering work (anything that changes what is drawn or how: `render.ts`, the shaders, a runner's skin, armour or pieces, `soldier.ts`, `body.ts`, a model, a map's scenery): measure before and after as `docs/PERFORMANCE.md` says (How to measure), the user, 2026-11-16. The CPU of a match frame with the touched classes in it, on Highline and on Tranquility; the first draw of what was added; the vertices at every level (far, near, close). A cost that went up is fixed in the same change (the rules of `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`: levels of detail, build once, the store, no allocation per vertex) until it is back at the numbers before; only what cannot be won back goes to the user, as a size budget does. Put the numbers before and after in the PR.

## Git, PRs and releases

- Same flow as air-hockey: branch from `master` (`feature/…`, `fix/…`), one release label per PR, rebase on `origin/master` with `--force-with-lease`, conventional commit subjects in English.
- The PR's label picks the bump (`release:major`, `release:minor`, `release:patch`); `release:skip` publishes nothing. A merge releases at once, with no approval (single maintainer): npm publish, `vX.Y.Z` tag, GitHub Release, built from the PR's merge commit, one release at a time.
- Every PR description shows a screenshot of the game with the change (before and after for a visual fix), taken as in Tests (the built client in headless Chromium, or the browser pane). `gh` cannot upload an image: push it to the orphan branch `screenshots` as `<branch>/<name>.png` and embed it with its `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/korial29/tiny-fps/refs/heads/screenshots/…` link (the repo is public). Never commit a screenshot to `master`.

## Working with several agents

- The shared contract (types, constants, signatures) is committed first.
- Each agent gets its own worktree from that commit and owns its files; merge by hand, run every check, then look at a screenshot.
