# Rooftop CTF

A new direction, set by the user on 2026-11-05: an open-air capture-the-flag game between two teams of players, inspired by Mirror's Edge. The parkour movement and the character come first. There are no CPU enemies, no campaign levels and no weapons: a carrier is stopped by a tackle.

[PARITY.md](PARITY.md) and [GRAPHICS.md](GRAPHICS.md) record the work that came before. The renderer (lights, shadows, bloom, materials), the netcode (prediction, lag compensation), the sound and the HUD carry over.

Budgets: client 40 KB gzip, local 16 KB.

## The game

- **Map**: one symmetric rooftop map under an open sky. Two team bases on opposite towers, with routes between them at several heights: rooftops, gaps, ledges, ramps, pipes and cranes. Falling off the roofs sends you back to your base.
- **Teams**: red and blue, balanced as players join, up to 8 a side.
- **Capture the flag**:
  - Touch the enemy flag to take it.
  - Carry it home to your own flag while that one is at its base, and you score.
  - A dropped flag returns home after a few seconds, or at once when a teammate touches it.
  - A match ends at a score limit or a time limit.
- **Movement**, the heart of the game:
  - momentum (acceleration, air control, friction);
  - sprint and dash, the dash with a cooldown;
  - double jump;
  - ledge grab, then climb up;
  - wall-run along a wall, and slide under an obstacle;
  - coyote time and jump buffering, so jumps feel fair.
- **Look**: clean white concrete and glass with strong colour accents, and "runner vision" (red on the edges and objects you can use). A bright sky and a sun with sharp shadows, a hazy city skyline, bloom.

## Decisions

- A carrier is stopped by a tackle; there are no weapons (the user, 2026-11-05).
- Wall-run and slide come in step 3, with the other moves (the user, 2026-11-05).
- Step 6 rules, chosen by the agent that built them: a match ends at 3 captures or 480 s and a level score at the time limit is a draw (no overtime); teams are not rebalanced when players leave, a joiner fills the smaller side; a flag dropped by a fall or a leave lies where the carrier last stood on a floor, and one dropped by a tackle lies under him, which he cannot take back for 2 s.
- Step 7 rules, chosen by the agent that built them: a dash (its burst) or a slide into an enemy carrier tackles him, as the tackler drew him (`rewind()` with the input's `view`), with 0.3 m of reach on top of touching; the carrier drops the flag and stumbles for 0.8 s (no sprint, dash or jump, 2.5 m/s at most, thrown 6 m/s along the tackler, about a metre); a tackler waits 0.5 s between tackles. Only enemy carriers are tackled: a short stumble on anyone would turn the move into a general shove and take the flag out of it. Because the tackler stands by the flag he made drop, the step 6 rule sends it home at once (a teammate touching a lying flag returns it): a tackle on the ground resets the carrier's run, it does not leave the flag lying. Change that in `ctf.ts` only if the user wants the flag to lie.
- Step 8 rules, chosen by the agent that built them: runner vision is decided from the brushes alone (`vision.ts`, no map data). A top edge of a box is banded red when the ground beside it (the highest top under a point 0.4 m out, a taller brush there blocking it) is more than `STEP_H` and at most the grab range (2.4 m) below, and the edge is at least 1.5 m long; a brush whose two smaller sides are at most 0.6 m wide is a pipe or a beam and is red all over (lit); a free-standing brush at least 8 m long and 2.5 m high and at most 3 m thick gets a red stripe at hand height (1.2 to 1.45 m) on its two long faces. A roof edge over a void is no ledge, so the roofs themselves stay white, except the 2.4 m face of C over B. The skyline is two rings of seeded towers (`skyline.ts`) around the play area, drawn only; the renderer adds them to its own copy of the world (`city()`), so `build()` and the physics never see them. Each team's base gets a painted square (`zones()`), the map's team colour inside a frame.
- Step 9 rules, chosen by the agent that built them: the map is `HIGHLINE`, 212 brushes in a box 94 m wide and 240 m long, written as red's half (z 120 to 240) plus what stands on the centre line, and flipped across z = 120 in code, so the symmetry holds by construction and a test pins it (a red tint flips to blue, every other tint stays). Each base is a 26 m tower (40 by 28 m, glass) with the flag, eight spawns and two crates and a low wall for cover, 208 m from the other one. Three routes: the **Spine** (west, roofs at 26 m: an 11 m gap that takes a dash or a double jump, the **viaduct**, a 17 m gap along a wall that only a wall-run plus a dash crosses, and a 0.5 m beam over the plaza, about 25 s base to base), **Midtown** (centre, roofs at 19 and 21 m: gaps of 4 to 6 m and ledges to grab, a ladder of three platforms up the tower's face, about 36 s) and the **Lowline** (east, 14.5 m: a 24 m stair down the tower's east face, gaps of 4 m, a sign to slide under with a way round it, about 39 s, and walkable end to end with no sprint). They cross at the plaza (15 m, a dais, a glass canopy, cover) and at two shortcuts: a stair between the Lowline and Midtown's forecourt, and ledges from the forecourt up to the first Spine roof. A miss costs time: under the first gap and the viaduct a roof and a ladder of ledges lead back up, and the plaza under the beam has four platforms up to the Spine and Midtown's roofs have one to the plaza. Only a fall into the street (the kill floor at -25 m) sends a player back to his tower. The sun stands low in the east, along the short side, so the light is the same for both teams; the haze is thinner (0.0035) for the 200 m view.
- A ledge the hands can catch must be at least 2 m above the floor it is caught from when the two touch, since the hanging body needs 2 m under the top: the ledges of the ladders are 2.2 m or more, or a metre away from the roof under them (the last step to a roof is a jump of 1 m).
